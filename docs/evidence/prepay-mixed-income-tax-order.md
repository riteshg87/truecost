## What the enacted provisions establish

- **Section 196:** qualifying STCG on STT-paid equity shares, equity-oriented
  funds or business trusts is computed as 20% of the qualifying gain plus tax
  on the total-income balance as if it were total income. For a resident
  individual/HUF below the maximum exemption limit on that balance, the
  shortfall reduces the STCG before the 20% calculation. Chapter VIII
  deductions are allowed from gross total income reduced by that STCG
  (s.196(1)-(2), (4)).
- **Section 198:** qualifying long-term equity gains require STT conditions.
  Tax is 12.5% on qualifying LTCG above the separate ₹1,25,000
  allowance, plus ordinary tax on total income reduced by that LTCG. A
  resident individual/HUF basic-exemption shortfall reduces the LTCG before
  that calculation. Chapter VIII deductions are from gross total income
  reduced by the gain, and section 156 rebate is applied to income-tax after
  reducing tax on that LTCG (s.198(2)-(3), (6)-(7)).
- The **₹1,25,000 LTCG allowance** and the **total-income rebate tests** are
  separate quantities. Section 156(1) covers a resident individual with total
  income up to ₹5,00,000, capped at ₹12,500. Section 156(2) covers section
  202(1) total income up to ₹12,00,000, capped at ₹60,000 or marginal relief;
  section 156(3) caps that rebate at section 202(1) tax. Section 198(7)
  explicitly removes LTCG tax before applying the rebate. Section 196 has no
  matching special rebate sentence.
- Finance Act section 3(4) applies surcharge thresholds using total income
  including sections 196-198 gains, while capping surcharge on tax computed
  for that gain component at 15%. Section 3(15) adds 4% cess to income-tax
  plus surcharge after those calculations. The same component split is needed
  for old-regime Paragraph F and for new-regime section 202.

## Source-supported arithmetic fixtures

Assume resident individual, no surcharge, no other income, losses or
deductions, and qualifying STT facts.

1. **New-regime STCG:** ordinary income ₹3,00,000 and section 196 STCG
   ₹2,00,000. The section 202 basic exemption is ₹4,00,000, so the ₹1,00,000
   shortfall reduces the gain; ₹1,00,000 × 20% = ₹20,000 special tax. The
   remaining ordinary amount is below the basic exemption, so pre-rebate tax
   is ₹20,000. The general rebate allocation is deliberately not included.
2. **New-regime LTCG:** ordinary income ₹5,00,000 and section 198 LTCG
   ₹2,00,000. No basic-exemption adjustment applies; ₹75,000 above the
   ₹1,25,000 allowance × 12.5% = ₹9,375. Ordinary section 202 tax on
   ₹5,00,000 is ₹5,000; section 156(2)/198(7) rebates that ₹5,000, leaving
   ₹9,375, plus 4% cess = ₹9,750.

## Unresolved reviewer questions

1. When both section 196 STCG and section 198 LTCG occur, what exact order or
   shared pool applies to the resident basic-exemption shortfall? The two
   sections describe their own reduced-gain calculation; they do not state a
   priority or allocation rule. Do not spend the same exemption twice.
2. For both gains, does the Chapter VIII deduction reduce gross total income
   once by the combined special gains, or does either section's wording impose
   a sequential order? Confirm before applying loan deductions.
3. Does section 156 rebate in a mixed 196+198 case remove both special tax
   components, only the section 198 component explicitly named in section
   198(7), or follow a separate allocation? Confirm separately for old-regime
   section 156(1) and new-regime section 156(2)/(3).
4. Confirm surcharge attribution when the combined gains cross ₹50 lakh, ₹1
   crore or ₹2 crore: total-income threshold inclusion is explicit, but the
   component allocation and marginal-relief presentation must preserve the
   15% cap without applying one rate to all tax.

Until these questions are answered and reviewed, mixed-income results remain
unsupported.
